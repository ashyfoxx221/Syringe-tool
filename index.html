Foundational Anatomy of a Syringe

Clinical Note (Alberta Nursing Curriculum Standards): Maintaining absolute sterility of specific syringe components during preparation is a key competency. While the barrel exterior, flange, and thumb rest are touch-safe, the inner plunger shaft, rubber stopper seal, needle hub base, and needle core must remain completely sterile to prevent systemic patient contamination during parenteral medication administration loops.

1. Needle Hub / Tip

The connector socket that mounts the needle assembly securely onto the forward syringe barrel tip nozzle core via a secure lock pattern.

2. Rubber Stopper

The black synthetic piston seal tip inside the barrel cylinder. Dosages are measured strictly along the forward geometric edge line.

3. Scale (Graduations)

The printed volume measurement marking increments stamped along the plastic barrel wall matching exact brand increments.

4. Barrel

The clear outer cylindrical plastic holding chamber that securely contains the fluid medication volume before discharge cycles.

5. Flange

The wide plastic wing tabs at the rear of the syringe body barrel. Serves as a dual finger-anchor grip foundation.

6. Plunger (Shaft)

The interior structural piston rod mechanism connected directly to the rubber seal. This shaft must remain sterile.

7. Thumb Rest

The broad plastic pad disk located at the outermost structural end of the plunger assembly. Grab here to manipulate volume.
